# Style brief: medieval

Status: **proposed** (assistant, 2026-10-25; for John). Style file:
`planner-core/src/main/resources/styles/medieval.json`. Biomes: none of its
own (picked by name or settings, not by biome).

## Precedents

Northern European towns of about 1300-1600: English Wealden and town houses
(Chester Rows, Shrewsbury), German and Alsatian Fachwerk (Rothenburg,
Colmar), Flemish market towns. Timber frame on a stone plinth, upper floors
jettied over the street, steep tiled roofs, a market square with a hall and a
church, walls with gatehouses.

## Materials

Frame dark oak logs; infill white terracotta (daub) and pale stone; plinth
cobblestone or stone bricks; roof bricks or mud bricks (tile). One frame and
one infill colour per building. Wealthy buildings, halls and churches: stone
bricks, ashlar, with timber only above. Interiors: spruce and dark oak, stone
bricks, red, brown and green cloth.

## Proportions and massing

Narrow street fronts (5-9), deep plots, 2-4 storeys, each upper storey
jettied 1 over the street. L and T plans, wings lower than the main range.
Ground floor of shops a block taller. Close studding or square panels with
posts every 3-4 blocks; braces only in end panels.

## Roof

Gable (gable end to the street in town, eaves to the street in the country),
1:1 or steeper, 1-block overhang, dormers on street slopes, cross gables over
wings. Chimney stacks of brick or stone.

## Windows and doors

Small and taller than wide (1x2; 2x2 on wealthy fronts), mullioned in
groups, aligned over each other. Doors in a surround, often off-centre. Shop
fronts open boards under a jetty or an arcade (rows).

## Avoid

Large glass areas; flat roofs; symmetrical classical fronts on houses; mixed
timber colours; brick-red roofs on every building in a row (vary tile tone a
little); a frame on the ground floor of a stone-plinth house where the plinth
should run.
