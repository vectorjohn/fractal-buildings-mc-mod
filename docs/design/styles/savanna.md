# Style brief: savanna

Status: **proposed** (assistant, 2026-10-25; John asked for savanna as its
own style, 2026-10-25). Style file: none yet (ROADMAP 7.6); savanna uses
`desert.json` by fallback today (`biome-fallback.json`). Biomes: savanna,
savanna plateau, windswept savanna.

## Precedents

African savanna and Sahel building: Sahelian mud-brick towns (Djenné,
Timbuktu) with buttressed walls and wooden beam ends sticking out; Hausa
compounds (Kano, Zaria) with flat earth roofs and relief-decorated walls;
round and square huts with steep thatch roofs across East and Southern
Africa; Ndebele houses with painted geometric bands; the vanilla savanna
village (acacia and dyed terracotta). Compounds of several small buildings
round a swept yard, shade first, low walls of earth.

## Materials

Walls of packed mud and mud bricks, or terracotta in earth tones (plain,
orange, brown; white terracotta as lime-washed render with one coloured band
at the base or eaves, Ndebele-style). Acacia logs as posts, beams and
projecting beam ends (fine at house scale; DS-1 bars them over 6 storeys),
acacia planks for doors, shutters and verandas, acacia fences round yards.
Roofs of hay bales (thatch) or acacia; a mud-brick or red sandstone plinth.
Town and city: terracotta, mud bricks, red sandstone, granite trim; tall
buildings in terracotta and brick (DS-1). Savanna is an open biome: fewer
wood-walled buildings than in forests (DS-29). Interiors acacia and
stripped acacia, terracotta floors, orange, yellow, brown and red cloth,
hay, potted dead bushes or acacia saplings.

## Proportions and massing

1-2 storeys, small footprints in groups: a compound of 2-4 small blocks
round a yard rather than one large house. Thick walls, a shaded veranda on
posts along the yard side. Round plans would suit huts (not built yet).

## Roof

Two families, chosen per settlement: steep thatch (hip, 1:1 or steeper,
1-block overhang; conical on round huts later) in villages and hamlets;
flat earth roofs with a low parapet and beam ends below it in towns and
cities (the Sahel look). No gables on stone or brick buildings.

## Windows and doors

Small openings, few on the street side; wooden shutters (trapdoors) rather
than much glass; doors under a deep lintel or the veranda. Kitchen may be a
separate small building in the compound (later).

## Avoid

The pale sandstone look (that is desert); cool grey stone walls; large glass;
pine-forest woods (spruce, dark oak) as walls; mixing several terracotta
colours on one face (one wall colour plus one band, DS-2); bright concrete.
