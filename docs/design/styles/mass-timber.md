# Style brief: mass timber

Status: **proposed** (assistant, 2026-10-25, from John's points of
2026-10-25; revised after the architect review of 2026-10-25). Style file:
none yet (ROADMAP 7.7). Biomes: the heavily
forested ones (DS-29: forests, taigas, jungles, cherry grove, pale garden,
dappled forest), for mid-rise and tall buildings in towns, cities and
metropolises; elsewhere rarely (agent's call). The tall-wood answer to
[DS-1](../STANDARDS.md): the only style whose walls may be wood over 6
storeys, and never over 20.

## Precedents

Modern engineered-timber buildings (CLT panels, glulam columns and beams):
Ascent, Milwaukee (25 storeys, the tallest, glass over a timber frame);
Mjøstårnet, Brumunddal (exposed glulam with diagonal bracing); HoHo Wien,
Vienna; Sara Kulturhus, Skellefteå; Brock Commons, Vancouver; Haut,
Amsterdam ([Dezeen, 2023](https://www.dezeen.com/2023/03/29/worlds-tallest-buildings-mass-timber-revolution/)).
Clean, manufactured, regular: it should never read as a log cabin stretched
upwards. Real CLT and glulam towers are near monochrome.

## Materials

Walls of planks (panels) with stripped wood (`stripped_*_wood`, grain on
every face) as columns, beams and floor bands; no logs with bark. One wall
species per building (at least per facade), picked by biome:

| Biome | Wall | Frame and trim |
|---|---|---|
| taiga, old-growth taigas | spruce planks | stripped spruce or stripped oak |
| forest, flower forest | oak planks | stripped oak or stripped spruce |
| birch forests | birch planks | stripped birch |
| dark forest | spruce planks | stripped spruce; dark oak only as a thin frame |
| pale garden | pale oak planks | stripped pale oak |
| cherry grove | birch planks | cherry as trim only (a 15-storey pink tower reads as a toy; confirmed by John 2026-10-26) |
| dappled forest | birch planks (poplar once checked in game) | stripped birch |
| jungles | patterned bamboo, or jungle planks (as tame as oak; `timber` in the block notes, John 2026-10-26) | stripped bamboo or stripped jungle |

**Frame one tone from the wall.** The frame is the same or a neighbouring
species (stripped birch on birch planks, stripped oak on spruce), never a
strong contrast: dark stripped wood on pale planks every few blocks and
every floor is Tudor half-timbering at 20 storeys. The frame reads by depth
and rhythm, not colour: columns and floor bands 1 block proud of a recessed
infill, or glass set back.

Bamboo only as a deliberate pattern (DS-1): bamboo planks or mosaic laid
uniformly, or stripped bamboo blocks in a repeating pattern of orientations
(vertical, horizontal, alternating panels); natural fit in jungles. Base: a
1-2 storey stone or concrete plinth (smooth stone, polished andesite, light
grey or white concrete) where the street is busy, else wood down to a stone
footing. Glass panes in a regular grid, about 35-50 % of the shaft facade
(agent's number), so it reads as an office or apartment tower, not a plank
box with holes. Glass-dominant facades (curtain walls over a visible timber
frame, the Ascent look) are a later addition. Iron bars or glass as balcony
railings. Interiors: exposed plank ceilings and stripped wood columns, light
floors.

## Proportions and massing

7-20 storeys, counted above ground (a tall ground floor counts as one; 20
storeys is about 85 blocks). Lower buildings may use it in forested cities
too; under 7 storeys that needs no DS-1 exception. Slender towers or
perimeter blocks on a regular structural bay (3-4 blocks); floor lines
expressed as bands; setbacks with roof terraces; balconies on upper floors
(DS-28). Optional: exposed diagonal bracing on one face.

## Roof

Flat, with a low parapet or a timber fascia; green roofs (moss, grass,
azaleas) and roof terraces welcome; plant room or stair core as a small box.
Leaves on a roof must be placed persistent (they decay otherwise) and moss
carpet sits on moss blocks: a roof garden that decays is a visible bug.

## Windows and doors

Large, regular: vertical strips per bay or ribbon windows per floor, stacked
storey to storey (DS-25), or a declared checkerboard alternation (DS-25
exception). Glazed ground-floor entrance under a timber canopy.

## Avoid

Logs and bark; rustic timber framing or a strongly contrasting frame (that
is medieval half-timbering); random or mixed woods and bamboo on one face,
including a wall list hashed per block (DS-2); dark oak as the main wall
(reads as burnt); pink cherry walls; more than 20 storeys of wood; timber
walls on the ground floor of a busy street without a stone base.
