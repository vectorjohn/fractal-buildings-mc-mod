# Style brief: mass timber

Status: **proposed** (assistant, 2026-10-25, from John's points of
2026-10-25). Style file: none yet (ROADMAP 7.7). Biomes: the heavily
forested ones (DS-29: forests, taigas, jungles, cherry grove, pale garden,
dappled forest), for mid-rise and tall buildings in towns, cities and
metropolises; elsewhere rarely (agent's call). The tall-wood answer to
[DS-1](../STANDARDS.md): the only style whose walls may be wood over 6
storeys, and never over 20.

## Precedents

Modern engineered-timber buildings (CLT panels, glulam columns and beams):
Ascent, Milwaukee (25 storeys, the tallest, glass over a timber frame);
Mjøstårnet, Brumunddal (exposed glulam with diagonal bracing); HoHo Wien,
Vienna; Sara Kulturhus, Skellefteå; Brock Commons, Vancouver; Haut,
Amsterdam ([Dezeen, 2023](https://www.dezeen.com/2023/03/29/worlds-tallest-buildings-mass-timber-revolution/)).
Clean, manufactured, regular: it should never read as a log cabin stretched
upwards.

## Materials

Walls of planks (panels) with stripped wood (`stripped_*_wood`, grain on
every face) as columns, beams and floor bands; no logs with bark. One wall
species per facade, picked by biome from the woods the block reference marks
`timber`: spruce (taiga), birch (birch forest), oak (forest), pale oak (pale
garden); a frame in a contrasting stripped wood. Bamboo only as a deliberate
pattern (DS-1): bamboo planks or mosaic laid uniformly, or stripped bamboo
blocks in a repeating pattern of orientations (vertical, horizontal,
alternating panels); natural fit in jungles. Base: a 1-2 storey stone or
concrete plinth (smooth stone, polished andesite, light grey or white
concrete) where the street is busy, else wood down to a stone footing.
Glass panes in a regular grid. Glass-dominant facades (curtain walls over a
visible timber frame, the Ascent look) are a later addition. Iron bars or
glass as balcony railings. Interiors: exposed plank ceilings and stripped
wood columns, light floors.

## Proportions and massing

7-20 storeys (lower buildings may use it in forested cities too); slender
towers or perimeter blocks on a regular structural bay (3-4 blocks); floor
lines expressed as bands; setbacks with roof terraces; balconies on upper
floors (DS-28). Optional: exposed diagonal bracing on one face.

## Roof

Flat, with a low parapet or a timber fascia; green roofs (moss, grass,
azaleas) and roof terraces welcome; plant room or stair core as a small box.

## Windows and doors

Large, regular: vertical strips per bay or ribbon windows per floor, stacked
storey to storey (DS-25), or a declared checkerboard alternation (DS-25
exception). Glazed ground-floor entrance under a timber canopy.

## Avoid

Logs and bark; rustic timber framing (that is medieval); random or mixed
woods and bamboo on one face (DS-2); dark oak as the main wall (reads as
burnt); more than 20 storeys of wood; timber walls on the ground floor of a
busy street without a stone base.
