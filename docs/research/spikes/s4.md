# S4 Region map

## Question

Does the region layer (2D hashed Voronoi cells, per-cell city decision, biome as a multiplier, F2−F1 margins) place cities sensibly against real vanilla biomes? Which cell size, and how can a city span more than one cell? (Coordinator addendum, ADR 0002: John wants effectively unbounded cities; the open question is "grow cells or join cells".)

## Result

**Yes, with two design findings.**

1. **Site-only decisions lose most cities.** A cell's site lands in water or a river about half the time, so deciding at the site alone gave 26–41 cities per window at cell size 1024. Choosing the best of 8 hashed candidate centres inside the cell (still a pure function of the cell and the coarse probe) gave 73–96. Keep the "evaluated once per cell" rule, but evaluate a small fixed candidate set, not one point.
2. **A city is only as big as its cell lets it be, unless cells join.** With cities kept inside their own Voronoi cell, mean radius is about 0.14 S to 0.22 S (141 at S=512, 214–238 at 1024, 223–269 at 2048). A radius-900+ city needs S of roughly 5000. Letting a big city annex neighbouring cells (below) removes that limit at no cost to the field.

Sensible placement held on all four seeds (window 24,576 blocks, 12,288 radius, about 58% land):

- 0 city centres in water, rivers or peaks (asserted per city), 0 cities with more than 15% water or peak in the footprint.
- Spacing: nearest city centres are at least 0.3 S apart by construction (centre kept 0.15 S from the cell edge; asserted). Observed nearest-neighbour mean is about 1.0 S to 1.2 S at S ≤ 1024.
- Cities straddling biome groups: 23–37% of cities at S=1024 (two palette groups of at least 20% each), 5–35% at 2048. Big joined cities straddle far more (40–80% of radius ≥ 900, small n).
- Per cell: 55–60% rejected as water (the world is 42% water), 2–4% peak or unsuitable, about 20% lose the roll. About 22–28% of all cells hold a city.

## Design for unbounded cities (feeds the worldgen ADR)

- **Do not grow cells.** At S=4096 the window holds 2–3 cities; to fit a radius-1000 city inside its own cell S has to be about 5000–7000 (about 25–50 km² per cell). Cities stay rare and uniformly sized.
- **Join cells.** Keep S=1024, jitter 0.7. A cell's hashed type and radius (no probe needed) say how many neighbours it claims: every cell whose site is within `radius` of the centre. A claimed cell cannot hold its own city. Conflict rule: higher hash priority wins; the loser is dropped (not shrunk), so the answer stays a pure function.
- **Cost of joining is small.** "Is cell X claimed?" needs the type/radius hash of the ~49 cells within the largest radius (2,400 × 1.25 = 3,000 blocks, 7×7 cells at S=1024), and a probe evaluation only for the few whose type is large. The decision is per cell and cacheable.
- **Small settlements ignore the join.** About 70% of cells draw village or town (radius ≤ 500), which fit one cell.
- Needed in the field design: a per-cell hashed priority; `radius` independent of the cell (not clamped to it); the candidate-centre set; the claimed-cell lookup.

## Cost (derived, not measured live)

Grid fill: 147k columns in 4–5 s on 8 threads (about 0.25 ms per column including height and a primed biome lookup). One cell decision samples at most about 50 footprint points × 8 candidates, about 0.1 s uncached on the live probe; a cache per cell (as ARCHITECTURE says) makes it a one-off.

## Dump format (proposed fixture spec, `fb-region-dump/1`)

JSON, byte-stable (fixed `%.3f`, ordered lists). Top level: `format`, `seed` (64-bit), `mc`, `grid {x0,z0,step=64,nx,nz}`, `palette[]` (biome paths, first-seen order), row-major arrays `biome[]` (palette index), `surface[]` (WORLD_SURFACE height), `water[]` (surface − floor), then `layers[]`: `cellSize, jitter, mode, policy, sites[[cx,cz,x,z]…], cells[{cx,cz,siteX,siteZ,city,reason,type,centreX,centreZ,radius,radiusFit,edgeDist,roll,chance,suitability,siteSuitability,candidate,water,peak,groups{}}]`.

- The grid is on the 64-block lattice and the planner snaps every probe to it, so **a recorded grid replays any decision exactly**. `GridData` doubles as the `SurfaceProbe` for the CLI and viewer.
- Size: 384² grid is 2.1 MB per seed (410 KB gzipped). For fixtures use a 192² window (about 0.5 MB) or gzip.
- Command: `/fbregion dump [radius]` writes `<server dir>/fb-region/seed-N.json` off the server thread.

## Gotchas

- `ChunkGenerator.getBaseHeight(OCEAN_FLOOR_WG)` returned the minimum Y for every column in the plain JUnit bootstrap (no server, `blocksMotion` false there), though it matched real ground in game ([terrain-biome-queries](../worldgen/terrain-biome-queries.md)). The spike reads `getBaseColumn` instead (first non-air = surface, first non-air non-fluid = floor). Do the same in unit tests, or test the heightmap only in game.
- **Priming was not exercised.** With the priming lookup removed, the 36,864-sample grid test still showed 0 differences between 1 and 8 threads, so this test cannot catch a missing prime (ties are about 1 in 2,000 at quart resolution; 64-block steps rarely hit one). Keep the priming (cheap) and test it at quart resolution when implementing for real.
- The biome list at the surface Y still contains `dripstone_caves` and `lush_caves` in a few samples (3D biomes where the surface is carved); they score as the default 0.4. Not fixed.
- Voronoi cells are irregular: `F2−F1` is only a lower bound of twice the edge distance, so the spike uses the exact distance to the bisectors (`edgeDistance`).
- Not tried: domain warp of the cell lookup (ARCHITECTURE says warped); the biome multiplier table is hand-set; no Terralith/Tectonic run (vanilla only).

## How to re-implement properly

Port `RegionField`, `Mix64`, `RegionPlanner` to `planner-core` with a `SurfaceProbe` and `BiomeClass` driven by biome tags (`c:` convention), add a per-cell join rule, and keep `GridData`/`RegionDump` as the recorded-probe fixture. The mod's `RegionSampling.fill` is the live probe.
