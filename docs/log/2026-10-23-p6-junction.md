# P6 JUNCTION: street heights where streets meet (round 1: rule and diagnosis; round 2: the planner fix)

Round 1 result: **partial** (round 2 below finishes it). The `junction-height` rule exists, is tested, and is measured on every set; the planner is unchanged
(no golden or digest moved), because the fix is a redesign of how a street's height is decided, not a contained patch.

## What landed

- `SettlementChecks.cityJunctionHeights(plans)` (rule id `junction-height`) and `SettlementChecks.junctions(plans)` (the per-pair
  data): every cell covered by two *different* streets (a street end inside another's footprint, or a crossing) must have surfaces within
  `JUNCTION_STEP` = 1 block. One violation per pair of street ids, with the first bad cell, both levels, how many shared cells are
  off and the largest difference. Pieces of one street in neighbouring tiles share an id and are never compared with each other; plans are
  sorted by district id, so the result is independent of the order of the plans (tested).
- `city-check --junction` turns it on (and `--list N` lists N violations per site instead of 20). **Off by default**, so the
  existing "city-check 0 with no `--ignore`" bar of every other item keeps holding; it is not in `CityPlanner.check`. Turn it on
  by default in the same change that fixes the planner.
- `JunctionHeightTest` (5 tests, hand-made plans: end on a street at another level fails, crossing within 1 passes, sloped street whose footprint spans
  more than 1 fails, same street in two tiles, plan order, streets that do not touch). Mutation: `JUNCTION_STEP = 100` fails 2 of them.
- `GroundWalkTest.junctionStepsDoNotGetWorse`: javadoc numbers fixed to what the test measures (670 tiles, 370 with a step over 1, 229 over 3; B3's
  log and the brief quoted 376/234 and 377/221) and the ratchet tightened to exactly that. It cannot go to 0 until the planner is fixed.

## Diagnosis: why a contained fix does not exist

1. **Every street's height is a function of that street alone.** `Districts.pieces` grades a whole street once (`Grading.profile` over its whole column list,
   anchors every 8 columns from its own start, median of three ground samples, clamped to 1 in 3 by repeated passes) and each tile keeps its slice. That is what makes two
   tiles agree on a street (ROADS). Nothing knows another street's height. Two streets that cross get the heights their own anchors and clamps give.
2. **At a lattice corner four segments end on one node.** Example, gate-temperate-city (1104,-9600; ground 85): `a07_m9600` ends at 83, `a07_m9744` at 80, `a1m60_1104` at 83,
   `a1m60_960` at 97 (it has been climbing at the 1 in 3 limit from 108). The end anchor starts as the ground height but the clamp moves it by up to 2 per 8 columns
   from the next anchor, so segments that come in on different slopes disagree.
3. **Pinning the ends is not enough (tried, reverted).** Pinning both ends of every whole street to the exact ground at the end column (the existing `Grading.profile(.., pins)` mechanism that gate
   passages use) cut W-W pairs over 3 from 226 to 121 but left 619 of 641 W-W pairs above 1 and added 2 `road-grade` failures (two pinned ends too close for the grade limit). The
   reason: a junction is a *footprint* (9 wide for an arterial), overlapping streets must be **flat over the whole overlap** (radius = half the widest street plus one), not equal at the centre column; a street
   climbing 1 in 3 differs by 2 to 3 blocks over 9 columns even if both centres agree.
4. **Crossings that are not at a lattice node** (skeleton main roads over anything, grid lines over border streets, `sd` streets and lanes ending on a street, quays) are not
   known to either street: finding them needs the other street's columns, and the other street is, in general, not even in the tile that grades this one (a grid line runs `GRID_REACH` beyond its tile).
5. **The border problem.** A pin that moves the profile moves it for `3 x |change|` columns (the 1 in 3 limit). A tile sees only streets within its own tile plus `MARGIN` = 8; a node 20 blocks inside
   tile A is invisible to tile B, but its ramp can reach B's columns. So pins must be found by a window wider than `MARGIN + ramp` (up to 3 x 20 = 60+), or the road has a step at the tile border, which the
   B3 gametest forbids (`tile-border-agree` is 2D only and would not notice).
6. **Not every pair can be satisfied by pinning.** Two nodes closer than `3 x |height difference|` along one street have no profile that meets both within the grade limit; the brief's options
   (ramp over a longer run, end before the junction with steps, drop the minor street) all need a decision per street that every tile makes the same way, and dropping a street feeds back into
   `roads-connected`, lot frontage and the rooted-component pass.

## What the fix needs (recommended design, one item of its own)

- A **junction node field owned by the settlement's `RoadNetwork`** (memoised like `network()`), not by the tile that plans first: nodes = lattice corners, crossings of skeleton roads with lattice
  segments and with each other (all geometry, no terrain), each with one height `J(node)` that is a pure function of the exact ground near the node (so any tile computes the same value), made grade-feasible
  between neighbouring nodes by a deterministic relaxation over the node graph (only node positions are probed: a metropolis has a few hundred).
- Whole streets get **flat pin zones** (radius = half the widest street meeting + 1) at their nodes, through `Grading.profile(.., pins)`; priority main/path > arterial > border street > grid > lane (a lower street takes the
  higher street's level at the node and ramps over `3 x difference`).
- Grid lines and tile-local streets (`sd`, lanes, quays, links) take the level of the street they end on from that street's final profile in the tile (available there), after the whole streets.
- Tile consistency: node and ramp discovery in a window of tile + `MARGIN` + the longest ramp, with a cap on the ramp (a node whose ramp would exceed it is left to the drop rule), and a test that grades a street from two tiles
  and compares columns across the border (extend `GroundWalkTest`, which already counts steps across the border).
- Then: `junction-height` on by default in `CityPlanner.plan`, `GroundWalkTest` limits to 0, goldens/digests for every affected district re-baselined, 2 to 3 before/after pictures.

Risks: the node relaxation and the drop rule change which streets exist, so most plan digests move; this wants to be its own phase-6 item with B2's digest re-baseline after it.

# Round 2 (lead-coder): network-owned junction heights, rule on by default

Result: **done**. `junction-height` is part of `CityPlanner.check` (so of `city-check` and every test that asserts a clean city); 0 on every set, no `--ignore`.

## Design (what landed)

- **`JunctionField`** (settlement package, memoised per source and exact probe in `SkeletonSource.Direct`, warmed by `WorldPlanner.warm`). It
  enumerates the settlement's *whole* streets the way district detail draws them (skeleton main roads and paths, lattice arterials and border streets,
  grid-line runs over the planned box plus `GRID_REACH`), finds every **contact**: a column of one street whose footprint overlaps another's. A run of
  contact columns along a street is a **pad**; pads joined through contacts form one **junction** with one level, pinned flat on every pad
  (`Grading.profile(.., pins)`), so every shared cell has the same surface on both streets. Gate passages are pads with a fixed level.
- Junction **levels**: median exact ground over the pads, made grade-feasible along every street: between neighbouring pads a street can rise at most
  the sum of `gap / 3` over the grader's anchors there (every 8th column, span ends, pinned columns). Level = midpoint of the largest feasible field
  under the ground and the smallest over it (two multi-source shortest-path sweeps, order-independent), then raised to bridge-deck floors (a pin never
  lowers a deck; `Grading` now lets a pin raise one) and clamped to the gate levels. The field grades every whole street once; tiles take their pieces'
  heights from it (`JunctionField.profile`), so a grid run cut by a tile's window gets the same heights as in its neighbour.
- **Other streets meet what is already there** (`Districts.meetPins`): subdivision streets, lanes, links and ports are graded with pins where their
  footprint overlaps a graded street of the tile or a quay: per street they touch, one level within a block of all that street's cells on the run
  (two runs the grade cannot join count as one). **Drop rule**: a street whose pins cannot be honoured (too steep between two streets, two streets more
  than 2 apart at one column, or a bridge deck lifting it) is `unmet` and not drawn (port stubs too).
- **Quays** are graded ahead of the tile's other streets (`quayWholes`, canonical order by smallest column): they meet whole streets through the field
  (`levelsAt`), earlier quays through their profiles. A stretch they cannot follow within the grade (a street beside it climbing faster) is cut, decided
  per stretch from the levels there, and parts shorter than a whole run are dropped; a quay column squeezed between two whole streets more than 2 apart
  is no quay (`JunctionField.splits`, pointwise). Links are laid after all quays.
- **Pieces of one whole street** (`r`, `r.1`: a road a square cuts) build a cell they share once, from the first piece (`DistrictScenes.ground`,
  `Grading.road(.., CellFilter)`), and the rule does not compare them.
- **Rule semantics changed**: each cell is judged in the plan of the tile that builds it (ground is clipped to the tile, `Pieces.ground`). Round 1
  compared a tile's margin pieces against the neighbour's streets, which no tile builds.

## Costs and surprises

- The field reads every whole street's terrain once, at the settlement's first tile: about 133k probe calls (3,968 chunks) for the 8-cell metropolis,
  15k (498 chunks) for a one-cell city; live that is roughly 7.5 s of chunk fills for the metropolis up front, spread before over its tiles. `warm()` builds it
  on the worker. `HubSquareGateTest`'s budget for the first tile went from 150k to 220k calls.
- Junctions on steep ground get cut and fill above 4 blocks (a level between the streets' grounds); it is a note (`needs more than 4 blocks of cut or fill`),
  not a rule. The badlands picture shows one such cut.

# Round 3 (lead-coder): streets that end against another street's side

The verifier found that touching footprints (a street ending a cell short of another's side) were no junction anywhere: 69 abutting pairs over 1 and 23 over 3 on the gate cities, about half of GroundWalkTest's remaining over-3 tiles.

- **Rule**: `junction-height` also compares, across one cell edge, a street's end face (cells its first or last `width / 2 + 1` columns claim) with another street's cell. An end on the tile window's edge is no end (the piece goes on in the neighbour). Pairs counted once with the overlap pairs.
- **Field**: two whole streets are in contact when their footprints overlap, or touch across a cell edge where one of them ends.
- **Meet pins**: a minor street (and a quay) also meets the cells beside its footprint at its end columns, and any street's end face beside its side. A column touching one street whose cells span more than 2 has no level that fits: a lane with one is not drawn, a quay stops short there.
- **Quays**: graded from their smaller end in canonical order (`canonicalQuays`; a window-dependent direction broke tile-border-agree once the cuts became common); each part left after a cut meets the street at its new end.
- **Last guard** (`trimAbuttingEnds`): a street whose end face still touches another at another level (a piece cut by this tile's own lot reservation, which the field cannot know of) loses end columns until it does not, inside the tile clear of its border band and never detaching it; if trimming would detach it, its end is brought to the other street's level with a 1-in-3 ramp (`relevelEnd`).
